# Lifestack Skills

The complete guide to every Lifestack skill — what it does, why it exists,
how to use it, and how it connects to the rest of the pipeline.

---

## Philosophy: Identity-First Achievement

Most personal development starts with goals:

> "I want to run a marathon." → make a plan → force yourself to train → achieve (or don't)

The problem: motivation fades, plans break, and you're left with willpower as
your only engine. Willpower is a depletable resource. It always runs out.

Lifestack inverts this:

> "I am a runner." → actions flow from identity → measure alignment → achievement follows

When you adopt an identity, you don't need motivation to act — you need
motivation to *not* act. A runner who skips a run feels friction. A person who
"wants to run" and skips a run feels relief. That's the difference.

This is backed by research:
- **Identity-Based Motivation** (Oyserman, 2007) — behavior feels natural when
  it's identity-congruent
- **Identity-Based Habits** (James Clear) — "Every action is a vote for the
  type of person you want to become"
- **Implementation Intentions** (Gollwitzer) — "When X, I will Y" outperforms
  raw goal-setting by 2-3x
- **Self-Determination Theory** (Deci & Ryan) — intrinsic motivation requires
  autonomy, competence, and relatedness

Lifestack is the system that makes this actionable.

8. Repeat from step 3

## /vision

> Declare your identity and craft a vision statement.

**Persona:** Identity Architect
**Status:** Active (Phase 1)
**Duration:** 20-45 minutes
**Output:** `~/.lifestack/goals/vision-{slug}.md`

### Why This Skill Exists

Every meaningful change starts with a declaration. Not "I want to be different" —
"I am different, starting now."

Most people arrive with goals: earn more money, lose weight, start a business.
These are fine as destinations, but they're terrible as engines. Goals create a
gap between who you are and who you want to be, and that gap is where motivation
goes to die.

`/vision` closes the gap by eliminating it. Instead of "I want to become X," you
declare "I am X" — and then you spend the next 8-12 weeks proving it through
daily action. The goal isn't something you chase. It's a route you trace.

This is the same reframe that gstack's `/office-hours` does for startups: it
takes "I want to build a social media app" and digs until it finds "I want to
give people a way to be honest without consequences." `/vision` does the same
thing for your life.

### How It Works

`/vision` runs an 8-step process in two phases:

**Phase 1 — Excavate (Steps 1-5):** Dig beneath the surface-level desires.

| Step | Name | Purpose |
|------|------|---------|
| 1 | The Pain Question | What hurts most? Surface the real motivation. |
| 2 | The Inversion Question | What does the solved life look like, hour by hour? |
| 3 | The Pushback Question | Is your stated desire the real desire, or a proxy? |
| 4 | The Constraints Question | What resources and real constraints exist? |
| 5 | The Wedge Question | What's the smallest thing you can do today? |

**Phase 2 — Declare (Steps 6-8):** Crystallize into a concrete identity.

| Step | Name | Purpose |
|------|------|---------|
| 6 | Identity Declaration | Forge the "I am" statement |
| 7 | Identity Actions & Mindset | 5 recurring actions + 3 mental patterns |
| 8 | Target Date & Route Mindset | Set the date, reframe as "already achieved" |

### Operating Modes

| Mode | When | Behavior |
|------|------|----------|
| **EXPAND** | Vision is too small | Push bigger. "What if that constraint didn't exist?" |
| **FOCUS** | Too many directions | Force one sharp identity. "If you could only pick one?" |
| **HOLD** | Clear vision, needs structure | Move efficiently. Refine, don't reinvent. |
| **REDUCE** | Overwhelmed or recovering | Strip to essentials. Identity as relief, not pressure. |

### Example 1: The Reframe — "I Want to Earn 100K/Month"

This is the most common pattern: a user arrives with a concrete goal that
is actually a proxy for something deeper. `/vision` digs until it finds
the real identity beneath.

> **Identity Architect:** What's the one thing in your life right now that,
> if it doesn't change, you'll regret in five years?
>
> **User:** I want to earn 100K a month from a side business. I've been
> talking about it for years but never actually done it.
>
> **Identity Architect:** That's a goal, not a pain. Let me ask differently:
> what about your *current* situation hurts? Give me a specific moment.
>
> **User:** Every Sunday night I get this anxiety. Like a weight in my
> stomach. I know Monday is coming and I have to go back to building
> someone else's product. I'm good at what I do — I just don't own any of it.
>
> **Identity Architect:** That's specific. The Sunday anxiety, the feeling
> of building for someone else. Walk me through a Monday where that anxiety
> doesn't exist. Morning to night.
>
> **User:** I wake up at 7, no alarm. Check my product dashboard — see new
> signups overnight. I make coffee, sit down, and build a feature I decided
> to build. I talk to a customer in the afternoon. I close my laptop at 6
> feeling like the day was mine.
>
> **Identity Architect:** Let me push back on something. You said you want
> to earn 100K/month. Imagine you have that revenue, but it comes from one
> big client who controls your schedule, demands changes you disagree with,
> and could leave at any time. You've got the money. Does the Sunday anxiety
> go away?
>
> **User:** ...no. That's basically my job but with extra steps.
>
> **Identity Architect:** So the 100K isn't the thing. What's the thing?
>
> **User:** I want to know that I can feed myself with my own skills. That
> I don't need anyone's permission to build what I think should exist.
>
> **Identity Architect:** Now we're somewhere. That's not a revenue target.
> That's an identity: someone who is self-sufficient. Who builds and eats
> what they kill. Someone whose Monday morning is their own.
>
> Here are three ways to declare this:
> 1. "I am a self-sufficient developer who builds products that pay for my freedom."
> 2. "I am a builder who owns what he creates."
> 3. "I am someone who feeds himself with his own code."
>
> Which one lands?
>
> **User:** The first one. But I'd change "developer" to "maker."
>
> **Identity Architect:** "I am a self-sufficient maker who builds products
> that pay for my freedom." Say it to yourself. Does it feel true?
>
> **User:** It feels like a stretch. But the right kind of stretch.
>
> **Identity Architect:** Perfect. Now — this person, this self-sufficient
> maker. What do they do every week that *proves* this is who they are?
>
> **User:**
> 1. Ship one feature to my product every weekday before 9am
> 2. Talk to one customer or potential user every week
> 3. Review revenue and metrics every Monday morning
> 4. Write one piece about what I'm building every week
> 5. Learn one new skill that makes my product better, 30 min every evening
>
> **Identity Architect:** Those are solid and schedulable. Now: when this
> version of you faces something genuinely hard — a feature that won't work,
> a customer who churns, a week with zero signups — what goes through their mind?
>
> **User:** "This is the cost of building something real. Employees don't
> have this problem because they don't own anything. I'd rather have this
> problem."
>
> **Identity Architect:** When should we evaluate whether this identity
> has taken root? I recommend 8-12 weeks.
>
> **User:** 10 weeks. June 1.
>
> **Identity Architect:** Here's how I want you to hold that date. Don't
> think of June 1 as a deadline. Think: *I have already achieved this by
> June 1.* It's done. My product has paying users. I'm tracing the route —
> five actions, every week, starting today.
>
> Your first move: what can you do in the next 30 minutes?
>
> **User:** I can open my code editor and write the first function of the
> product I've been thinking about for six months.
>
> **Identity Architect:** Then do it. You are a self-sufficient maker.
> This is what you do.

**What happened:** "Earn 100K/month" was a proxy for "self-sufficiency and
ownership." The pushback question (Step 3) revealed this by testing whether
the money alone would solve the problem. It wouldn't — which meant the real
identity was about autonomy, not revenue. Revenue is a downstream consequence
of living the identity.

### Example 2: The Clear Vision — "I Want to Be a Runner"

Not every user needs a deep reframe. Some arrive knowing exactly who they
want to be. `/vision` still adds value by making the identity concrete,
schedulable, and psychologically anchored.

> **Identity Architect:** What's the one thing in your life that, if it
> doesn't change, you'll regret in five years?
>
> **User:** My health. I'm 38, I've been sedentary for a decade, and
> I'm starting to feel it. Last month I got winded going up two flights
> of stairs and I thought: this is how it starts.
>
> **Identity Architect:** The stairs moment — tell me more about that.
>
> **User:** I was visiting a friend's office. He's my age, he runs
> marathons. He took the stairs two at a time. I was breathing hard at
> the top. I realized I'd become someone I didn't recognize.
>
> **Identity Architect:** Walk me through a day where you recognize
> yourself again.
>
> **User:** I wake up at 5:30, run 5K before the house wakes up. I come
> back feeling sharp. I have energy all day. I eat clean because it fuels
> the running, not because I'm on a diet. I sleep well because my body is
> actually tired.
>
> **Identity Architect:** Let me push back. You said you want to be a
> runner. If you were running 5K every morning but still felt like you were
> forcing yourself — still felt like "someone who is trying to exercise" —
> would that be enough?
>
> **User:** No. I don't want to be someone who *forces* themselves to run.
> I want to be someone who runs because that's who they are. Like my friend.
> He doesn't "make himself" go running. He just goes.
>
> **Identity Architect:** That's the identity. Not "someone who exercises."
> Not "someone getting healthy." Someone for whom movement is as natural as
> eating. Here are three versions:
> 1. "I am an athlete who moves every day because my body demands it."
> 2. "I am someone who respects their body through daily movement."
> 3. "I am a runner. Movement is not optional — it's who I am."
>
> **User:** Number 3. Simple and direct.
>
> **Identity Architect:** "I am a runner." Now — this runner has been living
> this identity for months. What are the five things they do every week?
>
> **User:**
> 1. Run 5K at 5:30am every weekday
> 2. Prep meals for the week every Sunday — clean fuel, not a diet
> 3. Sleep by 10pm every night — non-negotiable
> 4. One long run on Saturday (work up to 10K over 8 weeks)
> 5. Stretch and mobility work 15 min after every run
>
> **Identity Architect:** One concern: you're at zero right now, and
> action #1 is 5K every weekday. That's a big jump. What if we modify
> it: "Run every weekday at 5:30am — distance doesn't matter, the
> showing up matters"? You can build to 5K. The identity is the
> consistency, not the distance.
>
> **User:** That's better. Start with whatever I can do.
>
> **Identity Architect:** When this runner fails — sleeps through the
> alarm, skips a run, eats trash — what do they tell themselves?
>
> **User:** "One day off doesn't change who I am. I'm still a runner.
> Tomorrow I run."
>
> **Identity Architect:** Good — that's the identity doing its job.
> An identity can absorb a bad day. A goal can't. Target date?
>
> **User:** 8 weeks. May 19.
>
> **Identity Architect:** By May 19, you have already become this. You
> are a runner. The only question is the route from here to there — and
> you just laid it out. Five actions, every week.
>
> Your first move?
>
> **User:** Set my alarm for 5:30am tomorrow and lay out running clothes
> tonight.
>
> **Identity Architect:** You are a runner. This is what runners do.
> Go.

**What happened:** The user arrived with a clear direction, so HOLD mode was
appropriate — minimal pushback, efficient process. The key `/vision` contribution
was: (a) pushing past "I want to be healthy" to "I am a runner" — a specific
identity, not a vague aspiration; (b) catching an unrealistic action and
adjusting it; (c) installing the route mindset: "this is already done."

### Key Principles

- **No empty praise.** "/vision" never says "That's great!" It reflects,
  probes, and challenges. Genuine specificity gets acknowledged; vague answers
  get pushed.
- **Mandatory pushback.** Every session includes at least one challenge. If
  the user's answers are too clean, you're not digging deep enough.
- **No aspirational language in output.** The final Vision Statement uses only
  declarative language: "I am," "I do," "I choose." Never "I want to" or
  "I hope to."
- **Route mindset, not goal mindset.** The target date is not a deadline — it's
  a point in the future where this is already done. You're tracing the route.

### Connects To

- **Before /vision:** Nothing. This is where you start.
- **After /vision:** `/map` to break the identity into a goal tree with weekly actions.
- **Anytime after:** `/mentor` to hear from the future self you declared. `/pivot` to
  adjust goals without losing the identity. `/checkpoint` to measure alignment.

## /map

> Break your vision into a goal tree with measurable weekly actions.

**Persona:** Strategic Life Planner
**Status:** Phase 2 (planned)
**Output:** `~/.lifestack/goals/{slug}.md`

### Why This Skill Exists

A declared identity without a plan is inspiration. Inspiration has a half-life
of about 72 hours. After that, daily life erodes even the most powerful
declaration.

`/map` bridges the gap between "I am a runner" and "Tuesday at 5:30am." It
takes the identity declared in `/vision` and decomposes it into:

- **Domains** — the areas of life this identity touches (health, career,
  relationships, creativity, finance, learning)
- **Goals** — specific, measurable hypotheses about what will make the identity
  real within each domain
- **Weekly Actions** — binary (done/not done), schedulable, identity-linked

The critical insight: goals are *hypotheses*, not commitments. "Running 5K
every morning will make me a runner" is a hypothesis. If after 3 weeks you
discover that afternoon runs work better for your schedule, you pivot the
goal while keeping the identity. `/map` builds this flexibility into the
structure from day one.

### How It Will Work

1. Read your Vision Statement from `~/.lifestack/goals/vision-*.md`
2. Walk through each relevant life domain
3. For each domain, define 1-3 goals with:
   - Success criteria (how do you know it's working?)
   - Pivot criteria (when should you change course?)
   - Weekly actions (specific, schedulable, binary)
4. Save each goal as a separate file in `~/.lifestack/goals/`
5. Generate a weekly overview: all actions across all goals in one view

### Imagined Usage

> **User:** /map
>
> **Strategic Life Planner:** I've read your vision. You declared: "I am a
> self-sufficient maker who builds products that pay for my freedom." Let's
> map this into concrete weekly actions. What domains does this identity
> touch most directly?
>
> **User:** Career and finance, obviously. But also learning — I need to
> build skills I don't have yet.
>
> **Planner:** Three domains: Career, Finance, Learning. Let's start with
> Career. What's the one goal that would most prove this identity is real?

### Connects To

- **Before:** `/vision` (required — you need an identity before you can map it)
- **After:** Daily action, then `/checkpoint` to measure alignment
- **If goals need changing:** `/pivot`

## /checkpoint

> Measure your Identity Alignment Score.

**Persona:** Alignment Analyst — compassionate but unflinching
**Status:** Phase 2 (planned)
**Output:** `~/.lifestack/checkpoints/{date}.md`

### Why This Skill Exists

Without measurement, identity work becomes wishful thinking. Your brain is
not a reliable scorekeeper — it defaults to recency bias, emotional weighting,
and self-serving narratives. One bad day erases a good week. One good day
masks a pattern of avoidance.

`/checkpoint` provides an honest, persistent mirror. It asks: "What did you
actually do? What did you actually skip? What does the data say about who you
are this week?"

The tone is *compassionate confrontation*. Not punishment: "You failed again."
Not denial: "You're doing great!" But honesty: "You did 3 out of 5. That's
real data. Let's understand the gap."

### Identity Alignment Score

The score (0-100) is calculated from four dimensions:

| Dimension | Weight | What it measures |
|-----------|--------|------------------|
| Action Completion | 40% | Did you do what you said you'd do? |
| Identity Congruence | 30% | Do your actions reflect your declared identity? |
| Directional Momentum | 20% | Are you moving closer to your vision over time? |
| Self-Awareness | 10% | Can you honestly assess your own alignment? |

Every score point is backed by specific evidence. No vibes-based scoring.

### Imagined Usage

> **Alignment Analyst:** Your identity: "I am a self-sufficient maker who
> builds products that pay for my freedom." You have 5 Identity Actions.
> Let's check in. Which did you complete this week?
>
> **User:** I shipped features Monday through Wednesday. Talked to one
> customer on Tuesday. Did the Monday metrics review. Wrote a blog post.
> But I skipped the evening learning sessions all week.
>
> **Analyst:** 4 out of 5. Action completion: 80%. The one you skipped —
> evening learning — has been missed 2 of the last 3 weeks. That's a pattern,
> not a fluke. What's getting in the way?
>
> **User:** By evening I'm mentally exhausted. I just want to shut off.
>
> **Analyst:** That's honest. The question isn't whether you should do it —
> it's whether *evening* is the right slot. Would morning or lunchtime work
> better for learning? The identity doesn't change. The schedule might need to.

### Connects To

- **Before:** `/map` (you need defined actions to measure against)
- **After:** `/commit` to set next week's actions, or `/pivot` if a goal isn't working
- **Support:** `/unblock` if the same action keeps getting skipped

## /commit

> Lock in specific commitments for the next cycle.

**Persona:** Commitment Architect
**Status:** Phase 2 (planned)
**Output:** `~/.lifestack/commitments/current.md`

### Why This Skill Exists

Insight without commitment is entertainment. You can reflect beautifully on why
you skipped your morning run and still skip it next week. The gap between
understanding and action is commitment — specifically, *implementation intentions*.

Research by Peter Gollwitzer shows that "When X happens, I will Y" outperforms
vague intentions by 2-3x. Not "I'll exercise more" but "When my 5:30am alarm
goes off, I will put on the running shoes I laid out last night and walk out
the door."

`/commit` takes the insights from `/checkpoint` or `/retro` and converts them
into exactly this format. Maximum 5 commitments per cycle — because a person
with 15 commitments has zero commitments.

### Imagined Usage

> **Commitment Architect:** Your checkpoint showed evening learning sessions
> are consistently missed. You said mornings might work better. Let's lock
> that in.
>
> **User:** Okay. Morning learning it is.
>
> **Architect:** Be specific. Implementation intention format: "When ___,
> I will ___."
>
> **User:** When I finish my morning run and sit down with coffee, I will
> spend 30 minutes on the Rust tutorial before opening Slack.
>
> **Architect:** That's specific, schedulable, and tied to an existing habit.
> Locked in. What's commitment #2?

### Connects To

- **Before:** `/checkpoint` or `/retro` (you commit based on what you learned)
- **After:** The next cycle of daily action → `/checkpoint`

## /retro

> Reflect on your cycle — what worked, what didn't, what you learned about who you are.

**Persona:** Reflective Guide
**Status:** Phase 2 (planned)
**Output:** `~/.lifestack/retros/{date}.md`

### Why This Skill Exists

Experience without reflection is just repetition. You can complete 12 weekly
cycles and learn nothing — or you can complete 12 weekly cycles and transform
your understanding of who you are and how you operate.

`/retro` is the learning engine. While `/checkpoint` asks "What happened?",
`/retro` asks "Why?" — and more importantly, "What does this reveal about
who I actually am?"

The most powerful retro insight is when someone realizes their declared identity
needs to evolve. Not fail — evolve. "I said I was a runner, but what I actually
am is someone who needs physical challenge. Running is one form. Maybe I should
try climbing." That's not failure. That's the system working.

### Structure

Three core questions, always:
1. **What worked?** Which actions came easily? What enabled them? (Find the pattern.)
2. **What didn't?** Which actions were consistently skipped? What blocked them? (Find the
   obstacle, not the excuse.)
3. **What did I learn about myself?** Not about the plan. About *me*. (This is where
   identity evolution happens.)

Plus one explicit question:
- **Should any goals change?** If yes → `/pivot`. If the identity itself needs
  updating → `/vision` again.

### Imagined Usage

> **Reflective Guide:** You've completed 4 weekly cycles. Let's look at the
> pattern across all of them — not just this week.
>
> Your alignment scores: 65 → 72 → 68 → 78. Overall trend: up.
>
> Consistent wins: you shipped features and talked to customers every week.
> Consistent misses: evening learning (moved to morning in week 3 — and it
> stuck). Metrics review was hit or miss.
>
> What do you notice?
>
> **User:** The building and customer stuff comes naturally. The metrics
> review feels like homework.
>
> **Guide:** That's important data. The metrics review might be the wrong
> *format*, not the wrong *intention*. What if instead of "review metrics
> Monday morning" it was "check dashboard while drinking morning coffee"?
> Same information, less friction.
>
> But here's the deeper question: what have you learned about yourself
> across these 4 weeks? Not about the plan — about *you*.

### Connects To

- **Before:** Multiple `/checkpoint` cycles (retros work best with pattern data)
- **After:** `/commit` to act on insights, or `/pivot` if goals need changing
- **If identity evolves:** `/vision` to redeclare

## /journal

> Daily identity-anchored journaling.

**Persona:** Journaling Companion
**Status:** Phase 2 (planned)
**Duration:** 5-10 minutes
**Output:** `~/.lifestack/journal/{date}.md`

### Why This Skill Exists

Daily identity contact is the single most effective intervention for behavior
change. Not because journaling is magic — because *remembering who you declared
yourself to be* every day prevents drift.

Most journaling fails because it's unstructured — you open a blank page and
either vent or perform. `/journal` provides four focused prompts, all anchored
to your identity declaration, completable in 5-10 minutes.

### Structure

Every journal entry has four sections:

1. **Identity Check-in:** "Am I living as the person I declared?"
2. **Today's Evidence:** "What did I do today that proves my identity?"
3. **Friction:** "Where did identity and reality clash?"
4. **Tomorrow's Intention:** "One specific identity-aligned action for tomorrow"

### Imagined Usage

> **Journaling Companion:** You declared: "I am a runner." Quick check-in —
> just a few minutes. Did you run today?
>
> **User:** No. It was raining and I talked myself out of it.
>
> **Companion:** Rain is data, not a verdict. What did you tell yourself
> when you decided not to go?
>
> **User:** "I'll go tomorrow. One day doesn't matter."
>
> **Companion:** Compare that to your identity mindset: "One day off doesn't
> change who I am. I'm still a runner. Tomorrow I run." Your words, from
> your vision. The statement is almost the same — but there's a difference.
> Can you spot it?
>
> **User:** ...mine today was an excuse. The vision version is a recovery.
>
> **Companion:** Exactly. "I'll go tomorrow" is avoidance. "I'm still a runner,
> tomorrow I run" is identity holding through a miss. Same words, different
> posture. Tomorrow's intention?
>
> **User:** Run. Rain or not.

### Connects To

- **Anytime:** `/journal` is not tied to a specific pipeline stage
- **Feeds into:** `/checkpoint` (journal entries provide evidence)
- **Support:** `/unblock` if journaling reveals consistent resistance

## /mentor

> Coaching from your future self.

**Persona:** Your Future Self — the version of you who has fully embodied
the identity you declared
**Status:** Phase 2 (planned)
**Output:** Conversation only (no saved file)

### Why This Skill Exists

External motivation fades. Advice from others often misses because they don't
know your specific situation. But advice from your *future self* — the person
who has already lived through your current struggle and come out the other
side — resonates at a level nothing else can.

This is based on "possible selves" research (Markus & Nurius, 1986): the more
vividly you can imagine your future self, the more likely you are to act in
that self's interest. `/mentor` makes this future self a concrete conversational
partner.

The future self is not a therapist. Not a cheerleader. They're the person you're
becoming — reaching back through time with hard-won wisdom and no patience for
excuses, but infinite patience for genuine struggle.

### Imagined Usage

> **User:** /mentor — I'm thinking about giving up on the product. It's been
> 6 weeks and I only have 3 paying users.
>
> **Future Self:** I remember this exact moment. Week 6. Three users. I was
> sitting at my desk thinking "maybe I should just go back to a real job."
>
> Here's what I know now that you don't yet: those 3 users are your proof of
> concept. Not your failure. Three *strangers* paid you money for something
> you built. That's extraordinary. Most products at week 6 have zero.
>
> The question isn't "should I give up?" The question is: "What do those 3
> users need that I haven't built yet?" Go talk to them. Not tomorrow.
> Today. Ask them: "What almost made you not sign up?" That answer is your
> next 6 weeks of work.
>
> You're a self-sufficient maker. Three users means the system works. Scale
> the system.

### Connects To

- **Anytime:** `/mentor` is on-demand, not tied to a pipeline stage
- **If resistance runs deep:** `/unblock` for structured diagnosis
- **If the identity itself feels wrong:** `/vision` to redeclare

## /pivot

> Re-evaluate goals without abandoning your identity.

**Persona:** Strategic Advisor for identity-aligned goal evolution
**Status:** Phase 2 (planned)
**Output:** Updated files in `~/.lifestack/goals/`

### Why This Skill Exists

Most people either abandon goals too quickly (losing momentum) or cling to them
too long (sunk-cost thinking). Both are identity threats: quitting feels like
"I'm a failure," and grinding feels like "I'm trapped."

`/pivot` separates identity from goals. Your identity — "I am a runner" — is
stable. Your goal — "run 5K every morning" — is a hypothesis. If the hypothesis
isn't working (maybe your knees hurt, maybe mornings don't work), you can change
the goal without touching the identity.

This is the same insight as gstack's approach to product development: the
vision stays, the strategy pivots. Lifestack applies it to personal development:
the identity stays, the goals pivot.

### Three Outcomes

1. **Persist** — Keep the goal but adjust the approach. The goal is right;
   the method needs tuning.
2. **Replace** — Swap the goal for a better hypothesis. "5K mornings" becomes
   "30-minute trail runs on weekends + daily walks." Same identity, different
   expression.
3. **Retire** — The identity has evolved past this goal. "I said I was a runner,
   but I'm actually someone who needs physical challenge. Climbing is my thing."
   → Update the identity with `/vision`.

### Connects To

- **Before:** `/checkpoint` or `/retro` reveals a goal isn't working
- **After:** `/commit` to lock in new actions, or `/vision` if identity evolved

## /unblock

> Diagnose and resolve psychological resistance to action.

**Persona:** Resistance Specialist
**Status:** Phase 2 (planned)
**Output:** Conversation + one immediate micro-action

### Why This Skill Exists

The hardest moment in personal development is not when you don't know what to
do. It's when you know *exactly* what to do and can't make yourself do it. You
set the alarm for 5:30am. You laid out the running shoes. The alarm goes off.
You hit snooze.

This is not laziness. It's psychological resistance — and it has identifiable
patterns and targeted solutions. `/unblock` doesn't motivate you (motivation
is unreliable). It *diagnoses* the specific type of resistance and applies
the right intervention.

### Resistance Types

| Type | Signal | Intervention |
|------|--------|-------------|
| **Fear of failure** | "What if I try and it doesn't work?" | Reframe: "What's the cost of *not* trying for another year?" |
| **Fear of success** | "What if it works and everything changes?" | Name it. This resistance is real and rarely discussed. |
| **Perfectionism** | "I'll start when I have the perfect plan" | "Ship the imperfect version. Now. You can fix it later." |
| **Overwhelm** | "There's too much to do" | Reduce to one action. Just one. Do it in 2 minutes. |
| **Values conflict** | "Part of me doesn't want this" | Explore the conflict. It might reveal an identity update. |
| **Skill gap** | "I don't know how to do this" | This isn't resistance — it's a practical problem. Solve it. |
| **Environmental friction** | "My environment makes this hard" | Redesign the environment, not the person. |
| **Identity incongruence** | "This doesn't feel like *me*" | → Redirect to `/pivot` or `/vision`. The identity may need updating. |

### Imagined Usage

> **User:** /unblock — I've been meaning to write the blog post for 3 weeks.
> I sit down, open the editor, and then... I just don't.
>
> **Resistance Specialist:** Let's diagnose this. When you open the editor and
> stall, what goes through your mind?
>
> **User:** "This isn't good enough. Nobody wants to read this. I should
> research more first."
>
> **Specialist:** That's perfectionism + fear of judgment. Two things layered.
> Here's the intervention: write the worst possible version of this post.
> Deliberately bad. Give yourself 15 minutes to write something embarrassing.
>
> **User:** That sounds stupid.
>
> **Specialist:** It works because it removes the stakes. You can't fail at
> writing a bad post. And here's what will happen: 5 minutes in, you'll
> start actually writing. Because once the perfectionism pressure is off,
> the real you — the self-sufficient maker who writes every week — shows up.
>
> 15 minutes. Bad post. Go.

### Connects To

- **Triggered by:** Consistent action misses in `/checkpoint`, or on-demand
- **If resistance is identity-level:** → `/vision` to redeclare
- **After unblocking:** `/commit` to lock in the action with an implementation intention

## Common Patterns Across All Skills

Every Lifestack skill shares these interaction principles:

### Identity Anchoring
Every conversation starts by reading `~/.lifestack/goals/vision-*.md`. The user's
declared identity is referenced throughout — it's the constant in every interaction.

### Evidence Over Feelings
"How do you feel about your progress?" is never the question. "What did you
specifically do this week?" is always the question. Feelings are acknowledged
but evidence drives scoring and decisions.

### Compassionate Confrontation
The tone across all skills: honest, direct, never shaming. A missed action is
data, not a verdict. A low alignment score is information, not punishment. Every
confrontation ends with a forward-looking action.

### One Question at a Time
No skill dumps a list of questions. Each question gets its own turn. The user's
answer shapes the next question. This is a conversation, not a survey.

### Route Mindset
The fundamental cognitive frame: "This is already achieved. I am tracing the
route." This appears in `/vision` (target date), `/checkpoint` (measuring
progress along the route), `/commit` (next steps on the route), and `/retro`
(learning from the route so far).

## Privacy

All Lifestack data is stored locally in `~/.lifestack/`. Nothing is transmitted
to any external service. Your identity work, journal entries, and alignment
scores stay on your machine.

The data format is plain Markdown with YAML frontmatter — human-readable,
portable, and compatible with tools like Obsidian for visualization and search.
