# Lifestack Design Ethos

## Identity-First Achievement

The dominant model of personal development is goal-first:

1. Set a goal → 2. Make a plan → 3. Execute → 4. Achieve → 5. Become

Lifestack inverts this:

1. **Become** → 2. Align actions → 3. Measure alignment → 4. Achievement follows

This is not positive thinking. It is a cognitive reframe backed by research.
When someone says "I am a writer," their brain processes decisions differently
than when they say "I want to write a book." The identity frame activates
self-consistency bias — one of the most powerful forces in human psychology —
*in your favor*.

The goal is not to trick yourself. The goal is to choose who you are, then let
behavior follow identity instead of forcing behavior to precede identity.

## Four Design Principles

### 1. Identity-First

**Rule:** Always start with who, never with what.

**In practice:**
- `/vision` asks "Who are you becoming?" before "What do you want to achieve?"
- Every checkpoint measures identity alignment, not task completion
- Goals can change; identity evolves but does not flip

**Example:**
- Goal-first: "I want to lose 20 pounds" → fails when motivation drops
- Identity-first: "I am someone who respects their body" → decisions flow from this

**Why it works:** Identity-Based Motivation theory (Oyserman) shows that people
interpret difficulty differently based on identity framing. When effort feels
identity-congruent ("this is hard, but this is what someone like me does"),
people persist. When it feels identity-incongruent ("this is hard, maybe it's
not for me"), people quit.

### 2. Compassionate Confrontation

**Rule:** Be radically honest, never shaming.

**In practice:**
- `/checkpoint` says "You committed to 5 actions. You did 2. Let's talk about
  the gap" — not "You failed" and not "That's okay!"
- `/mentor` speaks as the user's future self: direct, loving, uncompromising
- Every confrontation ends with a forward-looking action

**Example:**
- Toxic positivity: "You're doing great! Don't worry about it!"
- Harsh judgment: "You failed again. You need more discipline."
- Compassionate confrontation: "You did 2 out of 5. That's real data. The 2 you
  did — what made those possible? The 3 you skipped — what got in the way?
  Let's use this to design a better week."

**Why it works:** Self-compassion research (Kristin Neff) shows that people who
treat setbacks with honest compassion — neither denial nor punishment — are more
likely to try again and more likely to succeed.

### 3. Metrics Over Vibes

**Rule:** Measure what matters. If you can't measure it, you can't manage it.

**In practice:**
- Identity Alignment Score: concrete number from 0-100
- Weekly action completion rate with specific evidence
- Trend lines over time, not single snapshots
- `/retro` demands evidence: "What specifically did you do?"

**Example:**
- Vibes: "I feel like I'm making progress"
- Metrics: "I did 4/5 identity-aligned actions this week. My alignment score
  went from 62 to 71. The action I skipped was morning meditation — I've
  skipped it 3 weeks in a row, which suggests the format isn't working."

**Why it works:** Without measurement, humans default to recency bias and mood.
A bad day erases a good week. Metrics provide an honest mirror that persists
across emotional states.

### 4. Goals Are Hypotheses

**Rule:** Goals are not sacred commitments. They are hypotheses about what will
bring you closer to your identity.

**In practice:**
- `/pivot` exists as a first-class skill, not an admission of failure
- Every goal includes success criteria AND pivot criteria
- `/retro` explicitly asks: "Should any goals change?"
- Identity is the constant; goals are the variables

**Example:**
- Fixed mindset: "I said I'd write a novel, so I must write a novel"
- Hypothesis mindset: "I said I'd write a novel because I believed that's how
  'a creative person' expresses themselves. After 4 weeks, I've discovered
  I express creativity better through short essays. Pivoting to essays is
  not failure — it's a more accurate hypothesis."

**Why it works:** Psychological flexibility (ACT therapy) is one of the strongest
predictors of well-being. Rigid goal attachment leads to sunk-cost thinking.
Treating goals as hypotheses preserves the identity while allowing the path
to evolve.

## Philosophical Comparison with gstack

| Dimension | gstack | Lifestack |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| **Core question** | "What should we ship?" | "Who am I becoming?" |
| **Unit of truth** | Working software | Aligned action |
| **Failure mode** | Shipped the wrong thing | Living the wrong identity |
| **Recovery** | Pivot the product | Pivot the goal, keep the identity |
| **Metric** | Velocity, quality | Identity Alignment Score |
| **Tone** | Direct, engineering-minded | Compassionate, evidence-based |
| **Time horizon** | Sprint (1-2 weeks) | Identity cycle (4-12 weeks) |
| **Inspiration** | YC, agile, lean startup | Psychology, philosophy, coaching |

They are the same pattern applied to different domains:
- gstack: Observe → Orient → Decide → Act → Ship → Learn
- Lifestack: Declare → Map → Act → Check → Adjust → Commit → Reflect
